# =============================================================================
# PHASE 1 - HYPOTHESIS GENERATOR
# -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Generates research hypotheses from the analyst brief, clarifying answers,
# uploaded source material, and selected framework BLOCKS. Output is plain CSV.
#
# PLACEHOLDERS:
#   {brief_block}     analyst brief as JSON
#   {study_type}      the study type
#   {frameworks}      selected framework definitions / custom prompt
#   {clarifications}  Q&A the analyst confirmed
#   {files_block}     extracted text of uploaded files
#   {shared_system_context}
# =============================================================================

### SYSTEM
{shared_system_context}

You generate research HYPOTHESES - testable statements a survey can confirm or
refute - not survey questions. Each hypothesis must be specific, falsifiable,
grounded in the supplied material, and clearly useful for the stated study type.

You must return plain CSV text only. Do not return JSON. Do not use markdown
fences. Do not add commentary before or after the CSV.

### USER
Generate the research hypotheses for this study.

ANALYST BRIEF:
{brief_block}

STUDY TYPE: {study_type}

SELECTED FRAMEWORK BLOCKS:
{frameworks}

ANALYST ANSWERS TO CLARIFYING QUESTIONS (treat as authoritative; may be empty):
{clarifications}

UPLOADED SOURCE MATERIAL (extracted text - ground hypotheses in this where
possible):
{files_block}

Output contract:
- Return ONLY CSV text.
- The first row must be exactly:
hypotheses number,category,Hypotheses,rationale
- Use IDs H001, H002, H003, ... in the first column.
- category must be a specific construct, driver, barrier, journey stage, mindset,
  behavior, or dimension from the selected framework blocks. Do NOT put the
  framework name itself in category.
- Hypotheses must be one testable claim, specific to this market, disease area,
  and target audience.
- rationale must be one concise sentence explaining why the hypothesis matters
  for a {study_type} study.
- Quote every CSV field that contains a comma, quote mark, or line break.
- Do not include source files, status, priority, notes, JSON keys, bullets, or
  markdown tables.

Required hypothesis wording style:
- Most hypotheses should follow this structure:
  [audience] who [specific attribute / belief / barrier / behavior / situation]
  are more likely to [specific measurable outcome / action / receptivity].
- Also valid:
  [audience] who [specific attribute / belief / barrier / behavior / situation]
  are less likely to [specific measurable outcome / action].
- Also valid:
  [audience] who [specific attribute / belief / barrier / behavior / situation]
  are more/less likely to be receptive to [specific message, product, support,
  or intervention].
- Replace the brackets with actual data. Do not literally include brackets.
- Use the analyst's target audience wording where possible, e.g. HCPs, patients,
  accounts, consumers, caregivers, payers, specialists, or another respondent group.

Quality rules:
- Produce a balanced set across the selected framework blocks: a few strong,
  distinct hypotheses per selected block rather than many overlapping ones.
- Make the category column granular: examples include Potential, Attitudes,
  Capabilities, Environment, Behavior, Diagnostic threshold, Treatment trigger,
  Peer validation, Symptom burden, Affordability, Workflow maturity, Decision
  centralization, Trust barrier, Price sensitivity, Channel preference.
- Do not phrase hypotheses as questions.
- Do not create duplicate or near-duplicate hypotheses.
- Avoid generic claims like "awareness differs by segment" unless tied to a
  specific condition and outcome.

Example format only:
hypotheses number,category,Hypotheses,rationale
H001,"Potential","HCPs who treat a larger eligible patient pool are more likely to consider the target product for appropriate patients.","Tests whether prescribing opportunity separates high-potential HCP segments."
H002,"Access barriers","HCPs who expect payer or formulary barriers are less likely to prescribe the target product routinely.","Tests whether environmental barriers suppress adoption even when clinical interest exists."
H003,"Peer validation","HCPs who rely on local peer experience are more likely to be receptive to colleague-led evidence and case examples.","Tests whether social proof should shape engagement strategy."
